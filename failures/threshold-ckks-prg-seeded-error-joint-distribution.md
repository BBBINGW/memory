## One primary falsifiable claim & cheap test
[HYPOTHESIS — CONDITIONALLY PASS] The shared use of one κ-bit random seed z to produce ell independently *specified* Ring-LWE error polynomials does **not by itself** invalidate the computational joint public/evaluation-key output distribution under Definition 2; if `G(U_κ) ≈_c U_m` is secure for the entire needed output length and `Sample(U_m)` is within negligible statistical distance η of `D_err^ell`, then for any PPT map `F` that consumes additional independent secret s and public coins A:
  `F(s,A, Sample(G(U_κ))) ≈_c F(s,A,E)`, E~D_err^ell.
[FALSIFIER] A polynomial-time distinguisher against the resulting **standalone keys** under the stated independence / hidden-seed assumptions would distinguish G(Uκ) from U_m or exploit sampler distance. Equivalently a standard PRG reduction proves the replacement; no encryption experiment necessary.
[MINIMAL TEST] Write a two-step hybrid; check what is given to DKG simulator and identify if the full transcript is in the image of an efficient post-processing map with independent auxiliary coins. Also test the easiest boundary when the seed itself is leaked. This is a formal symbolic proof, not a tiny simulation.

## Proof and its exact scope
[DERIVATION] Let s,A,coins be sampled independently of z; let `S:{0,1}^m->D_err^ell` be PPT sampling with `SD(S(U_m),D_err^ell)<=η`; for a secure PRG G let `Adv_G(t)` bound distinguishing G(Uκ) and U_m. Define efficient `F(s,A,E)` to form all `b_j=a_j*s+P*f_j(s)+e_j` and any other quantities derived *only* from s,A,E and independent coins. Hybrids:
H0=F(s,A,S(G(Uκ))),
H1=F(s,A,S(U_m)),
H2=F(s,A,E), E~D_err^ell.
Computational `Adv(H0,H1) <= Adv_G`; statistical `SD(H1,H2)<=η`; hence `Adv(H0,H2)<= Adv_G+η`.
This proves only the **standalone final key material** distribution computationally close (under independence, correct sampling, and no extra seed-dependent transcript). PRG raw outputs are NOT independently uniformly distributed statistically.
[SUPPORT BOUND] For seed κ and raw length m>κ, `SD(G(Uκ),U_m)>=1−2^(κ−m)` because PRG output's support has at most 2^κ points. For general independent error product distribution E, seed-derived support ≤2^κ and `SD(PRGerrors,E)≥1−2^κ*max_e Pr[E=e]`. This makes exact statistical identity generally impossible, but **does not contradict Definition2's computational objective**.
[LEAKAGE FALSIFIER] If a redesigned protocol explicitly reveals both z and the resulting error E, the polynomial-time distinguisher `[E==S(G(z))]` accepts generated world with probability 1, while in independent ideal world accepts only `Pr[E==S(G(z))]` (typically tiny). This is **not an attack on the author's DKG**: their adversary sees eval keys, NOT both the full seed and error coefficients, and an incomplete secret key generally prevents extracting errors. The witness only shows PRG security is not enough for arbitrary seed-dependent leakage/transcript views.
[NONBLACKBOX OBSTRUCTION] The **actual** two-round DKG potentially includes `Enc_tmp(z)`, `Eval(PRG,Enc_tmp(z))`, partial-decryption shares, and released evaluation-key outputs. These are correlated with the same z. They cannot automatically be modeled as F(s,A,Errors) with independent A; a simulator needs a new hybrid for encrypted seed and publicly or privately verifiable distributed-decryption consistency. Standard final-output postprocessing alone is insufficient. No proof of impossibility or distinguishing attack on this transcript is claimed.
[ADDITIONAL ASSUMPTIONS] Fresh seed independent from key and all other PRF inputs; a multi-output PRG / **strong PRF** secure on chosen deterministic labels (j,i) with domain separation; sampling transforms uniform bits into independently distributed designated error coefficients within negligible η; no public seed disclosure when its outputs become recoverable; FHE correctness and noise margin for encrypted sampler; circular security/auxiliary input of evaluation keys; security parameter vs m runtime. Weak PRF alone is insufficient for fixed public counter labels. These are assumptions, not verified components of §4.4.
