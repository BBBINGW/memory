## The minimal algebraic bridge (PASS)
[DEFINITION] For a prime exponent group order p, P in Mat_p(Z_t) is the p-cycle permutation matrix (P e_j=e_(j+1 mod p)). P^p=I; rho:z->P^z is injective and satisfies rho(a+b)=rho(a)rho(b), rho(a-b)=rho(a)(rho(b))^T. For GL n_mat >=p, use padded P=diag(P_p,I_(n_mat-p)).
[DERIVATION] GL integer Theorem 4.1 has two branches. Let
  U^(+)=U^(-)=P^a,
  V^(+)=P^b,
  V^(-)=(P^b)^T.
Then normalized outputs are C^(+) = (1/n_mat)P^(a+b) and C^(-) = (1/n_mat)P^(a-b). Multiplying by public n_mat (invertible modulo t) recovers both butterfly outputs in *one* native GL ct-ct matrix multiplication *if* the two input GL ciphertexts are prepared with exactly these branch layouts. This is not a free conversion from 2025/696 GSW keys. The relationship is valid at the plaintext algebra level; decryption correctness, noise and security after ct-ct operations not simulated.
[COMPUTATION] Choose p=5, n_mat=8, p_aux=3, t=97 prime (97 ≡1 mod 4*8*3=96). Explicit integer 8x8 matrices with five-cycle + three identity coordinates, test all a,b in [0,4]: 25/25 simultaneous normalized +/- outputs matched P^(a±b) mod97 exactly. Choose p=41,n_mat=64,p_aux=3,t=769 prime (769≡1 mod 768). 36/36 selected a,b in [0,5] similarly PASS. Both use source-admissible *plaintext matrix shape/root congruence*, not security parameters and not native FHE runs.

## Exact representation-size obstruction for THIS permutation bridge
[DERIVATION] In S_m, an element of prime order p must contain a cycle of length p, since each cycle length divides p. Hence faithful p-cycle permutation representation needs m>=p. GL supports n_mat a power of two, so for p=41, n_mat>=64. This lower bound is on *faithful permutation representations* only, not on all linear representations. Indeed a scalar primitive pth root in a suitable field is a smaller faithful character, but does not provide an inexpensive invertible encrypted return to the complete circulant GSW monomial representation.
[DERIVATION] The regular group-ring map phi: Z_Q[X]/(X^p−1)->Mat_p(Z_Q), f(X)->f(P), is injective (first column recovers coefficients), and image is the p-dimensional circulant algebra, not arbitrary p² matrix. Original 2025/696 already encrypts X^z in this compact group ring; converting to a generic dense matrix representation throws away this compactness. This does not prove a universal ciphertext lower bound or rule out specialized compressed matrix arithmetic.

## Source-format dimension and cost sanity check
[DERIVATION] For GL n_mat and odd p_aux, small RLWE ring R_q has 2 n_mat phi(p_aux) integer coefficient coordinates, and a matrix ciphertext is n_mat independent *two-polynomial RLWE ciphertexts*, giving 4 n_mat² phi(p_aux) ciphertext coefficient coordinates before CRT limbs/compression. For p=41,n_mat=64,p_aux=3, phi=2 => 32,768 coordinates for ONE GL ciphertext, supporting up to 2 phi=4 independent integer plaintext matrices. Even if ALL four available matrix batches are filled, raw ciphertext coefficients per represented matrix =8,192.
By contrast an ordinary rank-one GSW ciphertext with 2d gadget rows × 2 ring-polynomial components in the p=41 circulant group ring has 4 d p coefficients; at illustrative d=3, 492. This yields a rough coefficient-coordinate ratio 32,768/492 ≈ 66.6 for one GL ciphertext vs one GSW, or 8192/492≈16.65 at FULL 4-matrix GL batching. For toy p=5,n=8,p_aux=3 analogous counts are 512 vs 60. These are dimensional bookkeeping, NOT physical bytes, security-equivalent ciphertexts, or a rigorously fair performance comparison: original 2025/696 CRT/GSW parameters and moduli may differ and all auxiliary keys must be counted.
[FACT] Each GL ct-ct matrix product uses FOUR coefficient-matrix multiplications plus two Switchbig relinearization operations and carries a multiplication depth, whereas 2025/696 already has homomorphic_dot_product in its GSW ring and requires MANY output GSW monomials. Source 2025/1935 §3.5 shows two switching keys involving secrets s(Y^-1,W^-1) and s(X,W)s(Y^-1,W^-1), defined over extended R'_qqo; their memory/noise not zero.
[OPEN/BLOCKER] To feed initial 2025/696 ciphertexts Zi=GSW_Rtilde(X^-z_i) into GL native matrices, one needs a SOURCE-correct secure cross-ring/secret-key ciphertext conversion. To feed results into the next 2025/696 dot-product and MsgExtract, one needs conversion from GL encrypted P^z back to Rtilde GSW(X^z), not present in either source. No such low-cost conversion is constructed, proven, or benchmarked. Public plaintext linear conversion via reading first column does NOT suffice on GL ciphertext, because it is encrypted and the desired output is a GSW *gadget encryption* under another key.
[SECURITY] Matching toy t and native matrix dimensions does not imply acceptable 128-bit RLWE security, key-circular-security, DFR or comparable noise. The proposed padded permutation matrices have restricted plaintext distribution and different key format; cannot transfer either paper's security theorem verbatim.

## Novelty and decision
[FACT] Cyclic-permutation embeddings for bootstrapping are explicit prior art by 2014, and compressed monomial permutation matrices appear by 2023. Consequently neither rho(z)=P^z nor its butterfly composition is novel by itself.
[RESULT] PASS exact plaintext-group representation and simultaneous +/- branch trick, but FAIL proposed *cheap drop-in bridge*: original keys are not GL ciphertexts; the proposed representation expands into matrices and requires more expensive ct-ct matrix operations, extra large switching keys and unprovided bidirectional ciphertext conversion. At a conservative dimensional/cost gate, there is no evidence of an equal-security/DFR performance advantage. This is NOT a mathematical impossibility theorem for every cross-ring conversion, or a full cost lower bound proving every GL scheme slower.
[DECISION] PARK regular-representation GL hybrid. Do not continue tuning GL matrix dimensions for this particular conversion-only plan. Focus next on a same-primitive interface to avoid repeating source mismatch.
