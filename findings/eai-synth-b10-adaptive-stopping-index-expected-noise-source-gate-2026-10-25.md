## ONE minimal decisive calculation
[DERIVATION] Take independent Z1,Z2~Laplace(0,b), both E|Z_m|=b. A causal public-output-dependent index:
   J=1 if |Z1|>b; otherwise J=2.
Then P(|Z1|>b)=exp(-1), and memorylessness gives E[|Z1|;|Z1|>b]=2b/e. Since Z2 independent of Z1,
   E|Z_J|=2b/e + b(1-e^-1) = b(1+e^-1) = 1.367879441... b > b.
Thus "every fixed-index expected error <=b ⇒ chosen-index expected error<=b" is FALSE in general. This is a different issue from B5: B5 conditions on realized adaptive DATA and reuses noise, B10 concerns *selecting the stopping/prefix INDEX by previous releases* and taking a marginal expectation.
Sanity: E[max(|Z1|,|Z2|)]= (3/2)b by independent exponential order statistics. Hence E|Z_J|<=1.5b and the excess can be bounded by a conventional MAXIMAL inequality. More generally, if each of K fixed-index *noise-only* variables satisfies P(|Z_m|>a log(1/beta))<=beta for every beta∈(0,1), union bound gives P(max_{m<=K}|Z_m|>a(log K+u))<=exp(-u), and tail integration gives E max<=a(log K+1). With original Binary Mechanism fixed-index a=O(log^(3/2)K/eta), this yields a crude O(log^(5/2)K/eta) adaptive-index expected-noise bound provided one has a deterministic upper limit K on reachable subroutine indices or a valid tail bound on K. This is elementary existing mathematics, not a tight rate.
