## Exactly ONE primary hypothesis / cheapest decisive test
[HYPOTHESIS H-C02, FALSE] In a black-box IID candidate-generation regime with a known stationary but imperfect binary verifier (TPR s, FPR f with 0<f<s<1), the true rare-mode absence vs prevalence at least p can be certified with worst-case O(1/p) independent verifier calls at fixed two-sided error beta<1/2, matching a perfect verifier's 1/p scaling. The test is specifically existence/prevalence detection, NOT recovering true rare-mode examples or guaranteeing all future model generations retain them.
[ONE decisive test] Compare two black-box generator distributions H0:q=0 and H1:q=p. The only permitted observation from each independent generated candidate is the verifier's binary response. Compute the per-call Bernoulli KL divergence and use Pinsker + hypothesis-testing total variation to derive a necessary sample complexity. Inspect exact prior-art estimation/correction publications BEFORE claiming novelty.

## Precise assumptions and source provenance
[ASSUMPTIONS] Conditional distribution of verifier response given true rare label is fixed across sampled candidates, with sensitivity s=P(Y=1|rare)=TPR and false positive f=P(Y=1|nonrare)=FPR, both KNOWN without calibration expense. Independent candidate draws and conditionally independent verifier outputs; no access to candidate content, labels, generator distribution metadata, active prompting or feature side channels. These are favorable to the verifier and define a restrictive oracle-only test; cannot automatically generalize to complex prompt distributions, correlated raters or adaptive corpus curation.

## ONE exact two-world falsification [DERIVATION]
Let q be hidden true probability of rare class among IID generated candidates, 0<=q<=1. Let binary verifier response Y have
 a(q)=P(Y=1)= f+(s-f)q,
since conditionally on rare label the verifier returns positive with probability s, and otherwise f.
Thus H0:q=0 implies Yi~Bern(f); H1:q=p implies Yi~Bern(f+Delta*p), with Delta=s-f>0. From n IID verifier bits:
 D(P1^n || P0^n)=n d_KL(Bern(f+Delta*p)||Bern(f))
 <= n*(Delta*p)^2/[f(1-f)]
using KL<=chi-square for Bernoulli distributions (f∈(0,1)).
If one binary hypothesis test achieves type-I AND type-II error <=beta<1/2, then TV(P1^n,P0^n)>=1-2beta. Pinsker gives TV²<=D_KL/2; hence
 n >= 2*f*(1-f)*(1-2beta)^2 / [Delta²*p²].
For fixed beta,f,s with 0<f<s<1, this is Omega(p^-2) NECESSARY calls under this black-box model. This falsifies the hypothesized O(p^-1) guarantee; it is standard information-theoretic two-point testing, NOT a novel lower bound. If f=0,s=1, an all-negative sample is the only ambiguity under H1, and n>=ln(beta)/ln(1-p)=Theta(p^-1 log(1/beta)) suffices by an observed-rare-sample test. Do not directly substitute f=0 into Pinsker's derivation because KL/chi² denominators are singular.
Numeric: p=.001, f=.05, s=.90, beta=.05 => Delta=.85, a(H1)=.05085, Pinsker necessary n>=106,505.9 (integer 106,506); exact per-call KL d_KL(Bern(.05085)||Bern(.05))≈7.564779448e-6. More refined Bretagnolle–Huber yields necessary n>=ln(1/(4beta))/d_KL≈212,754.7 (integer 212,755) for *this comparison*; optional confirmatory check, not a separate research test. Perfect verifier n=ceil(ln(.05)/ln(.999))=2995 is sufficient. These numerical comparisons assume verifier error rates exactly known and stationary, independent output bits; actual pseudo-positive positive predictive value can be even worse when generator q becomes small.
Sufficiency benchmark for noisy oracle (NOT tested as another hypothesis): classic ACC prevalence estimator qhat=(Ybar-f)/Delta; Hoeffding gives |qhat-q|<=sqrt(ln(2/beta)/(2n))/Delta with 1-beta probability, so n=O(Delta^-2 p^-2 ln(1/beta)) suffices to distinguish q=0 vs q>=p in the oracle regime. Thus lower and simple known upper bounds match p^-2 order at fixed f,s,beta.

## Verdict / bounded inference
[NEGATIVE] H-C02 false under stated IID black-box verifier setting; nonzero FPR creates a p^-2 information-theoretic separation for rarity existence certification. Classical misclassification correction + quantification lower bounds in statistical literature are direct prior art; NOT a publication-worthy novel theorem.
[UNCLEAR] This is not a bound on guaranteeing an individual generated candidate's label, selected training-set future tail support, adaptive distribution shift, multi-silo DP privacy costs, or the actual Yi/Qiao training pipelines. It does NOT show all imperfect verifiers are hopeless: FPR may scale with p, richer verifier scores/multiple independent views can help, and raw candidates might be informative.
