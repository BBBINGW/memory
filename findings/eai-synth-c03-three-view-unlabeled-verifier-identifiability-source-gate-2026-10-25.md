## One decisive population-moment derivation [DERIVED]
Let true label H~Bern(q), 0<q<1, and three verifier outputs Y1,Y2,Y3∈{0,1} are conditionally *jointly* independent given H. Unknown true positive and false positive rates s_i=P(Yi=1|H=1), f_i=P(Yi=1|H=0). Assume Delta_i=s_i-f_i>0 for all i (known orientation, not known values).
Conditionally E[Yi|H]=f_i+Delta_i H. Therefore observable means mu_i=f_i+qDelta_i and observable pairwise covariances Cij=q(1-q)Delta_i Delta_j for distinct i,j. Conditional joint independence implies central third mixed moment
 M123=E[(Y1-mu1)(Y2-mu2)(Y3-mu3)]
     =q(1-q)(1-2q)Delta1 Delta2 Delta3.
Since all Cij>0, observed ratio
 R=M123/sqrt(C12*C13*C23)=(1-2q)/sqrt(q(1-q)).
Invert uniquely
 q=(1-R/sqrt(R^2+4))/2.
Then Delta1=sqrt(C12*C13/[q(1-q)*C23]), cyclic similarly for Delta2,Delta3; f_i=mu_i-q Delta_i and s_i=f_i+Delta_i. This is exact algebraic identification from the JOINT unlabeled distribution, no labeled calibration, with all assumptions explicit.
One numerical sanity check only: q=.02, f=(.1,.05,.08), s=(.9,.8,.85), Delta=(.8,.75,.77); observed means mu=(.116,.065,.0954), C12=.01176, C13=.0120736, C23=.011319, M123=.008692992. Ratio R=6.857142857...; inversion returns q=.02 and exactly original s,f to numerical precision. This is NOT a data experiment, simulation, model benchmark or new algorithm.
