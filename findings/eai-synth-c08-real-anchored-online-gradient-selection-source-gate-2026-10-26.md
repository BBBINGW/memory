## ONE decisive analytical transfer [DERIVED]
Let current parameters theta and real-task loss L_R(theta) have L-Lipschitz gradient (L-smooth) locally, let g_s=grad_theta ell(s;theta) be synthetic gradient and real gradient g_R=grad L_R(theta) (or estimate from current labeled real TRAINING mini-batch). A hypothetical SGD candidate update theta' = theta - eta*g_s has exactly by descent lemma:
   Delta_R=L_R(theta)-L_R(theta') >= eta <g_R,g_s> - (L eta²/2)||g_s||².
Also symmetric smoothness bound gives |Delta_R - eta<g_R,g_s>| <= (L eta²/2)||g_s||². Hence first-order score <g_R,g_s> is computable with REAL TRAINING gradients, without an external verifier/heldout validation. FROST §3.1/Appendix A.1 already instantiates u_{s,t}=<Rhat_t,g_{s,t}> using real-batch gradient EMA. This directly FALSIFIES the claimed necessity H-C08 in the online MIXED training regime.
The proxy's limits are in the paper: Appendix Eq16–17 includes reference e_t=R_t-Rhat_t and curvature/smoothness corrections. Lower-bound for batch gradient gbar is
   Delta_R,t >= eta U_t - eta||e_t|| ||gbar|| - (L eta²/2)||gbar||².
Thus mere positive U_t or maximum U_t does NOT guarantee improvement of true real-task loss or future generalization; FROST itself explicitly says large norms can reverse ranking. The current TRAINING gradient is a proxy for population real-task gradient and may have stochastic/selection bias; no DP or no-label-learning claim.
