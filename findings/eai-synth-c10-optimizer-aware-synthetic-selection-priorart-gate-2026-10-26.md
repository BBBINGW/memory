## ONE exact cheap 2D test (not a model benchmark)
Take true real-task objective L_R(theta)=.5||theta||_2² with theta=(1,1), g_R=(1,1), Hessian I (smoothness L=1). Fix local preconditioner D=diag(4,1) positive diagonal (representative frozen optimizer metric, not complete AdamW momentum/decay state); step eta=0.1. Two synthetic candidate gradients:
 g_A=(0,2), g_B=(1,0).
RAW alignment scores: <g_R,g_A>=2 > <g_R,g_B>=1, so optimizer-agnostic raw alignment selects A.
INDUCED alignment scores: <g_R,D g_A>=2 < <g_R,D g_B>=4, so preconditioned first-order utility selects B.
Exact one-step updates:
 theta'_A=theta−.1 D g_A=(1,.8), L_R(theta'_A)=.5(1+.64)=.82, true decrease Delta_A=1−.82=.18.
 theta'_B=theta−.1 D g_B=(.6,1), L_R(theta'_B)=.5(.36+1)=.68, true decrease Delta_B=1−.68=.32.
Hence the rankings are STRICTLY reversed even in exact smooth quadratic loss: raw ranks A>B but actual one-step decrease ranks B>A, B gains .14 more loss decrease (77.78% relative to .18), entirely under locally fixed D and eta. For general small eta, exact delta=eta<g_R,Dg>−(eta²/2)||Dg||²; here Delta_B−Delta_A=2eta−6eta²>0 for 0<eta<1/3.
No model-training experiment, no reward verifier, no finite-sample uncertainty; this is one deterministic analytic test that kills an overly strong rank-equivalence hypothesis. It does NOT prove a particular REAL FROST iteration misranks examples or that adding OPUS to FROST makes empirical synthetic-SFT gains. OPUS optimizer operator approximation itself is conditional; user has not authorized native testing.
