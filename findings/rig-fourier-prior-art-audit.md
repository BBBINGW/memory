## What overlaps [DERIVED]
RIG's special-root q=a^N+1 (a even, N power of 2, q prime) yields a primitive 2N-th root r=a and short evaluation coefficients (1,a,...,a^(N-1)). Nonuniform e(r) is the same fundamental small-numeric-residue / evaluation-bias mechanism already present in the older PLWE literature. The public Fourier/cosine test is a characteristic-function version of detection of such nonuniformity. However the old PLWE papers attack e(alpha) after subtracting a guessed secret contribution, while RIG's proposed symmetric scheme exposes shuffled error-only coordinates. They are NOT textually identical attacks; the difference is in scheme-specific instantiation, not an independently verified novel algebraic technique.

## Decisive mixture lemma [DERIVED]
Choose J uniformly among the d=N+n output positions of a RIG ciphertext. For any fixed unknown permutation pi, provided all N genuine-coordinate marginals are the same distribution D_b after suitable preprocessing, and padding is uniform U independent of bit b, the law of c_J is Q_b=p D_b+(1-p) U, p=N/(N+n). Since padding law is common, TV(Q_0,Q_1)=p*TV(D_0,D_1) EXACTLY. Likewise, for a bounded score h with E_{D_0}h-E_{D_1}h=delta, the mixed score difference equals p*delta. Permutation recovery is irrelevant to this one-coordinate test. This is elementary probability and follows regardless of how a fixed secret permutation is chosen, as J is uniform.
For the RIG t=2 attack, Fourier h=cos(2*pi*u*inv_2*c/q) has E_{Q0} h=p beta, E_{Q1} h=-p beta cos(pi v0/q) if v0 odd and coefficient noise symmetric, so randomized correct-bit rule has P_success=1/2+(p beta/4)*(1+cos(pi v0/q)). This is the user's previously verified one-challenge formula. The step from an available marginal distinguisher to an unknown-permutation attack is a direct mixture lift, with p>=1/2 for 0<=n<=N.
