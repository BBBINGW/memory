## Exact construction [DERIVATION]

Suppose a matching dense public key pk_s is distributed, capable of producing correct GLWE encryption at q0=2^52 (or at a higher Q whose ciphertext and compatible plaintext can be publicly reduced to q0). Choose publicly known message m, including m=0. Compute ct_s(m)=Enc_pk_s(m) using fresh public encryption randomness; decryption under dense s recovers m approximately. Then compute ct_t(m)=KeySwitch_D2S(ct_s(m)) using only the publicly published switching key. From correctness of D2S, Dec_t(ct_t(m)) ≈ m (mod q0), where t is windowed sparse secret. This **refutes H219** in the PK-enabled setting. No secret-key encryption oracle required. Whether bare SHIP-only key distribution includes pk_s is UNVERIFIED; do not silently assume it.

## Why these outputs are not automatically many independent standard RLWE samples [DERIVATION]

All switched CTs reuse ONE fixed public D2S GGLWE row, encrypted under sparse t at physical q=2^104, with unknown correlated dense-secret gadget plaintext; transformed q0=2^52 ciphertext outputs inherit KSK randomness, public pk randomness, input randomness and deterministic keyswitch linear/digit operations. Holding input encryption randomness fixed and varying m only adds a trivial public body plaintext, not another independent encryption sample. Fresh PK randomness can vary the outputs but they still share the *same fixed D2S key and PK*, and no proof has established i.i.d. uniform mask and independent bounded errors conditioned on public auxiliary data. Thus neither a clean q104 known-plaintext RLWE row nor unlimited independent q52 sparse RLWE samples follow from this algebra alone. An actual attacker game needs PK availability, mod-reduction requirements, chosen-input freedoms, published KSK matrix, noise, and sample correlation.
